# LLM-Judge
This article is a retrospective summary of a series of experiments I conducted on LLM-based autonomous driving evaluation. Rather than proposing a new model or algorithm, it documents several unexpected findings and methodological lessons encountered during the research process.

Across five studies, I observed how factors such as trivial baselines, training randomness, prompt wording, evaluation pipelines, and reference model strength could substantially influence experimental conclusions. Interestingly, one experiment also revealed that model confidence contained useful signals that were not reflected in its explicit predictions.

The purpose of this article is to share these empirical observations, reflect on what went wrong, and provide practical insights for designing more reliable LLM-based evaluation systems. It is intended as a research retrospective rather than a formal academic submission.
